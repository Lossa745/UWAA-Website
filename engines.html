Hybrid engine program

Building our own engines

Most student rocketry teams fly motors bought off the shelf. We are designing our own hybrid rocket engine instead, and members do the engineering: they design the injector and combustion chamber, build the oxidiser feed system and set up the test stand.

Status: in development, working towards our first static fire.

01 · What a hybrid is

How a hybrid engine works

A solid motor holds its fuel and oxidiser pre-mixed in one casting: light it and it burns to completion. A liquid engine feeds both propellants in as liquids, which adds significant plumbing complexity.

A hybrid sits between them. The fuel is a solid grain cast into the chamber; the oxidiser is stored separately as a liquid or gas and fed in through an injector. Because the two are never mixed until they meet in the chamber, the motor cannot sustain combustion on its own.

04 · Test campaigns

Tested before it flies

Before any engine is considered for a vehicle it will go through an instrumented static fire campaign, logging thrust, chamber pressure and feed pressures on every fire. Building that test capability is the program's current focus.

How a campaign runs

01

Cold flow

Oxidiser is run through the feed system and injector with no fuel grain loaded, to check flow rates and valve sequencing.

02

Short fires

Brief burns confirm ignition and the instrumentation, and chamber pressure is compared with predictions.

03

Full-duration fires

Full burns are repeated until the thrust curve is consistent from one fire to the next.

04

Review

Results are written up and checked against the design before the engine is considered for a vehicle.
